Resources

Validated measures, scales, and methodological materials developed by the lab. Citations link to the published articles; download buttons link to supplemental instruments and materials.

Beliefs About Seeking and Receiving Help

Scotney, V. S., & Tay, L. (2025). Beliefs about seeking and receiving help: A mixed-methods analysis. The Journal of Positive Psychology, 1–15. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2025.2549765

Supplemental Materials

Scotney and Tay (2025) used a mixed-methods approach to identify beliefs about seeking and receiving help and their relation to behaviors and well-being. Thematic Analysis of qualitative data identified five beliefs that were validated via factor analytic modeling: Help is Useful, the Help Process is Enjoyable, Help Diminishes Me, Help Threatens My Independence, and Helpers Can be Trusted. They called this belief taxonomy HELPS (Helpful, Enjoyable, Lessens, indePendence, and Safe). The relative importance of the HELPS beliefs in predicting help seeking, help receiving, and subjective well-being was examined via relative weights analysis using time-separated data. Safe and Enjoyable beliefs explained the most variance across different criteria.

Campus Well-Being Measure (CWBM)

Zhou, S., Weiss, H. A., McCuskey, B., & Tay, L. (2025). College student well-being: Explaining academic and behavioral outcomes from a representative college student sample. Journal of Happiness Studies, 26(5), 75. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-025-00906-3

Campus Well-Being Measure (CWBM)

Zhou et al. (2025) developed a 49-item novel holistic measure of student well-being, applied to two large samples of students to test its psychometric properties and assess predictive validity of academic and behavioral outcomes (e.g., grades, attendance at courses, student organization involvement). Relative importance analysis found that the grit theme in the well-being measure was the most important, followed by positive and negative SWB.

Developing Short Scales for SETPOINT Vocational Interest Dimensions

Hou, D. X., Su, R., & Tay, L. (2024). Measuring SETPOINT vocational interest dimensions: The development and validation of three short scales. Journal of Vocational Behavior, 149, 1–21. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2023.103959

SETPOINT-Based Short Interest Measures

Hou et al. (2024) developed and validated three short scales (8, 24, and 41 items) for assessing vocational interests based on the SETPOINT model, designed to capture the model’s eight dimensions with strong psychometric properties. The study found that the SETPOINT model outperformed RIASEC in predicting occupational membership, underscoring its practical utility in career assessment and research.

Mechanisms of Engagement in the Arts and Humanities Scales

Thapa, S., Vaziri, H., Shim, Y., Tay, L., & Pawelski, J. O. (2023). Development and validation of the Mechanisms of Engagement in the Arts and Humanities scales. Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts. https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000556

Mechanisms of Engagement — the RAISE Scale

Thapa et al. (2023) developed and validated scales measuring five theorized mechanisms through which engagement in the arts enhances well-being: reflection, acquisition, immersion, socialization, and expression. The measure demonstrated predictive and concurrent validity for flourishing outcomes, good test–retest reliability, and measurement equivalence across gender, between arts and humanities, and over time.

Italian Adaptation of the Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving for Children (CIT-Child)

Rita Andolfi, V., Tay, L., Confalonieri, E., & Traficante, D. (2017). Assessing well-being in children: Italian adaptation of the Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving for children (CIT-Child). TPM: Testing, Psychometrics, Methodology in Applied Psychology, 24(1), 127–145. https://doi.org/10.4473/TPM24.1.8

The Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving for Children (CIT-Child)

Andolfi et al. (2017) developed the CIT-Child to satisfy the need for measures that can evaluate child well-being. The metric was developed for Italian children based on the Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving (CIT) (Su, Tay, & Diener, 2014). Confirmatory factor analysis showed the goodness of fit of a 12-factor model.

The Attitudinal Learning Inventory

Watson, S. L., Watson, W. R., & Tay, L. (2018). The development and validation of the Attitudinal Learning Inventory (ALI): a measure of attitudinal learning and instruction. Educational Technology Research and Development, 66, 1601–1617. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-018-9625-7

Attitudinal Learning Inventory (ALI)

Watson et al. (2018) developed the ALI to measure a broad range of attitude constructs representing a holistic view of learning and instruction. The ALI comprises 15 scale items, validated across two samples totaling 1,009 participants, and conforms to a four-dimensional structure of attitudinal learning: cognitive, affective, behavioral, and social.

Subjective Quality of Leisure and Worker Well-being Measures

Kuykendall, L., Xue, L., Lei, X., Tay, L., Cheung, H. K., Kolze, M., Lindsey, A., Silvers, M., & Engelsted, L. (2017). Subjective quality of leisure & worker well-being: Validating measures & testing theory. Journal of Vocational Behavior, 103(2), 14–40. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2017.07.007

Leisure Affect ScaleLeisure Satisfaction Scale

Kuykendall et al. (2017) developed and validated the Leisure Affect and Leisure Satisfaction scales to measure the subjective quality of leisure. The scales have good internal consistency, fit well as unidimensional scales, are moderately stable, and exhibit construct validity through associations with measures of well-being in the work, family, and general life domains.

The Subjective Underemployment Scale

Allan, B., Tay, L., & Sterling, M. (2017). Journal of Vocational Behavior, 99, 93–106. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2017.01.001

The Subjective Underemployment Scale

Allan et al. (2017) developed the Subjective Underemployment Scale (SUS) to assess the six major domains of underemployment identified by Feldman (1996): pay, status, field, hours, involuntary temporary work, and poverty wage employment. The SUS demonstrated convergent validity by relating to similar constructs, work-related outcomes, and job attitudes.

SETPOINT: A Dimensional Model of Vocational Interests

Su, R., Tay, L., Liao, H.-Y., Zhang, Q., & Rounds, J. (2019). Toward a dimensional model of vocational interests. Journal of Applied Psychology, 104(5), 690–714. https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000373

Comprehensive Assessment of Basic Interests (CABIN) Scale

SETPOINT is an organizing framework for vocational interests and an empirically validated 8-dimensional model: Health Science, Creative Expression, Technology, People, Organization, Influence, Nature, and Things. This model proposes interests are structured hierarchically, from preferences for specific work activities at the lowest level, to basic interests at the intermediate level, to broad-band interest dimensions at the top.

CAPTION: A Lexically Derived Taxonomy of Psychological Situation Characteristics

Parrigon, S., Woo, S. E., Tay, L., & Wang, T. (2017). Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 112(4), 642–681. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000111

Supplemental Materials

Parrigon et al. (2017) conducted the largest lexical analysis of situational characteristics at the time, producing a clear 7-dimensional structure termed the “CAPTION” model: Complexity, Adversity, Positive Valence, Typicality, Importance, Humor, and Negative Valence. Both full- and short-form measures of CAPTION were introduced.

Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving and Brief Inventory of Thriving

Su, R., Tay, L., & Diener, E. (2014). Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being, 6, 251–279. https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12027

CIT Full InstrumentBIT Full Instrument

The Comprehensive Inventory of Thriving (CIT) offers a comprehensive well-being assessment for research and health-related contexts, such as clinical and psychiatric settings. The Brief Inventory of Thriving (BIT) provides a concise 10-item scale that offers a quick overview of core well-being dimensions, serving as a valuable tool for medical practitioners during initial intake.

A Practical Guide to IRT Measurement Equivalence Analysis

Tay, L., Meade, A. W., & Cao, M. (2015). An Overview and Practical Guide to IRT Measurement Equivalence Analysis. Organizational Research Methods, 18(1), 3–46. https://doi.org/10.1177/1094428114553062

Walkthrough IllustrationsIllustration 1Illustration 2Illustration 3

Tay et al. (2015) provide an overview and guide to implementing item response theory (IRT) measurement equivalence (ME) or differential item functioning (DIF) analysis, including three illustrations of two recommended IRT procedures and recommendations for conducting IRT ME/DIF.

A Conceptual and Methodological Framework for Psychometric Isomorphism

Tay, L., Woo, S. E., & Vermunt, J. K. (2014). A Conceptual and Methodological Framework for Psychometric Isomorphism: Validation of Multilevel Construct Measures. Organizational Research Methods, 17(1), 77–106. https://doi.org/10.1177/1094428113517008

Analytic Procedure for Testing IsomorphismMultilevel IRT & FA Item ParametersComparison of Multilevel IRT ModelsComparison of Multilevel FA Models

Tay et al. (2014) created a conceptual and methodological framework for understanding and testing various forms of isomorphism, explicating the different types of psychometric isomorphism, the conditions where it is appropriate, how it corresponds with different composition models and estimation methods, and the analytic procedures that can be used.
